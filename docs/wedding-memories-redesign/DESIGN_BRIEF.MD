# Wedding Memories — Design Brief

## 1. Project Overview

Wedding Memories is a mobile-first web application that allows wedding guests to capture, share and experience memories from a wedding reception.

QR codes will be displayed on tables throughout the wedding reception. Guests can scan these QR codes using their smartphones to access the application directly.

The application is intended to serve as a digital wedding guestbook combined with a disposable wedding camera, allowing the couple to experience their wedding through the perspectives of their guests.

### Existing Application

The current application supports:

- Capturing photographs using a smartphone camera.
- Sharing photographs with the wedding couple and other guests.
- Writing wedding wishes.
- Recording voice messages.
- Viewing personal contributions through My album.
- Viewing contributions from other guests through a shared gallery.
- Accessing Salam Kaut.
- Managing a limited photo allowance per guest.
- Managing pending and failed uploads.

The redesign must preserve these existing capabilities.

## 2. Project Objective

Redesign the existing home page to create a more intuitive, sophisticated and emotionally engaging wedding memories experience.

The application should feel purposefully designed for the wedding rather than resembling a conventional application with wedding-themed decorations.

The desired outcome is a bespoke digital keepsake that guests can easily use throughout the reception.

The redesign must improve:

- Visual hierarchy.
- Ease of participation.
- Mobile usability.
- Emotional engagement.
- Navigation clarity.
- Visual consistency.
- Perceived design quality.
- Communication of application states.

## 3. Target Audience

The application will be used by wedding guests with varying levels of technical familiarity, including:

- Young adults.
- Friends and colleagues.
- Family members.
- Parents and older relatives.
- Guests unfamiliar with the application.
- Guests accessing the application repeatedly throughout the reception.

Assume that most guests:

- Are accessing the application through a smartphone browser.
- Have never used the application before.
- Have not received instructions beyond scanning a QR code.
- Are distracted by conversations, food and wedding activities.
- May be operating their smartphones with one hand.
- May have inconsistent mobile data or Wi-Fi connectivity.
- Do not want to spend time learning how the application works.

The interface must accommodate these circumstances.

## 4. Wedding Brand Identity

### Wedding Invitation Reference

The existing wedding invitation is the primary brand reference for the application.

Preserve and reuse suitable existing branding assets, including:

- Wedding calligraphy.
- Botanical and floral illustrations.
- Wedding monogram, if available.
- Muted sage-green colours.
- Warm ivory backgrounds.
- Romantic editorial typography.
- Existing wedding-specific visual elements.

The redesign should be recognisable as belonging to the same wedding identity.

However, do not simply replicate the wedding invitation layout. The invitation and the application serve different purposes.

The invitation communicates event information.

The application encourages participation and enables guests to contribute memories.

Wedding information must therefore support the experience without dominating the interface.

## 5. Design Concept

### Modern Botanical Editorial

The visual direction combines the romance of botanical wedding stationery with the clarity of modern digital product design.

The aesthetic should draw inspiration from:

- Luxury wedding stationery.
- Fine-art wedding photography albums.
- Premium editorial publications.
- Boutique hospitality experiences.
- Minimalist botanical design.
- Thoughtfully designed digital keepsakes.

The desired visual characteristics are:

- Elegant.
- Intimate.
- Warm.
- Romantic.
- Refined.
- Timeless.
- Calm.
- Understated.
- Contemporary.

The interface should communicate quiet luxury through careful composition, typography, whitespace and restrained decorative details.

Premium quality should come from the quality of the design decisions rather than the quantity of visual embellishments.

## 6. Core Design Principles

### 6.1 Effortless Participation

Guests should understand what the application does and identify its primary action within a few seconds.

Minimise the number of steps required to capture or contribute a memory.

Avoid unnecessary registration, onboarding, tutorials and repeated form entry.

Do not require guests to understand the internal structure of the application.

### 6.2 Emotion Before Technology

Describe actions in terms of their human purpose rather than their technical implementation.

Examples:

- Prefer “Take a photo” over “Open camera”.
- Prefer “Record a message” over “Voice note”.
- Prefer “Your memories have been shared” over “Upload completed successfully”.

Emotional language must remain clear and must not obscure important functional information.

### 6.3 Clear Visual Hierarchy

Photo capture is the signature experience and should receive the highest visual prominence.

Writing wishes and recording voice messages are important secondary contribution methods.

The shared gallery encourages discovery and repeat engagement.

My album supports personal contribution management.

Salam Kaut is an optional feature and should not compete visually with the primary contribution actions.

### 6.4 Quiet Luxury

Use restrained colours, generous but purposeful whitespace, refined typography and subtle visual details.

Avoid visual noise.

Do not decorate every component simply because the application is wedding-themed.

### 6.5 Mobile-First Design

The primary experience is a smartphone web application accessed through a QR code.

Design for mobile screens first, then adapt the composition for larger displays.

Prioritise readable typography, comfortable touch targets, efficient vertical layouts and compatibility with mobile browsers.

### 6.6 Reliability Before Decoration

The application will be used during a live wedding reception.

A beautiful interface is unsuccessful if guests lose photographs, cannot submit wishes or encounter confusing upload failures.

Functional reliability must take precedence over decorative enhancements.

## 7. Experience Hierarchy

Organise the experience around three primary guest intentions.

### Capture

Guests want to take photographs and contribute memories.

This is the application's primary purpose.

### Express

Guests want to leave meaningful personal messages for the couple.

This includes written wishes and recorded voice messages.

### Discover

Guests want to see photographs and memories contributed by other guests.

This encourages social engagement and repeated use throughout the reception.

Supporting functions such as guest identification, upload management and Salam Kaut should remain accessible without disrupting these primary intentions.

## 8. Emotional Experience

The application should communicate that every guest has a unique perspective of the wedding.

Guests are not merely uploading photographs or submitting content. They are helping the couple preserve memories they may otherwise never experience.

The intended emotional message is:

“Capture the little moments and make them part of our forever.”

The application should make guests feel:

- Welcomed.
- Included.
- Appreciated.
- Encouraged to participate.
- Confident that their contributions have been received.

Avoid excessive sentimental language. Use warm and concise copy that feels personal and authentic.

## 9. Problems With the Existing Design

The existing home page has the following design shortcomings:

- The entire interface is enclosed within one large decorative card.
- Wedding information occupies too much vertical space.
- The interface resembles a wedding invitation with functional buttons added to it.
- Decorative typography is used too extensively for functional controls.
- Guest name entry remains permanently visible.
- Primary and secondary actions are not sufficiently differentiated.
- Related contribution methods are not grouped meaningfully.
- Gallery navigation is duplicated.
- All albums and QR card are prominently displayed despite being secondary to the guest experience.
- Photo allowance information is communicated redundantly.
- Upload feedback is presented as a technical paragraph.
- Active uploads and failed uploads are not clearly differentiated.
- Floral decorations compete with functional content.
- The shared gallery lacks visual and emotional prominence.

The redesign should resolve these problems through improved information architecture and visual composition rather than superficial styling changes.

## 10. What the Design Must Avoid

Do not introduce:

- Generic SaaS dashboard aesthetics.
- Generic AI-generated interface patterns.
- Excessive rounded containers.
- Nested decorative cards.
- Heavy drop shadows.
- Glassmorphism.
- Gold gradients.
- Excessive gradients of any kind.
- Large decorative icon containers without purpose.
- Excessive floral decorations.
- Distracting ornamental backgrounds.
- Unnecessary floating elements.
- Continuous decorative animations.
- Large headings that push functional actions unnecessarily below the viewport.
- Redundant navigation.
- Unnecessary guest registration.
- Artificial engagement statistics.
- Fabricated guest contributions.
- Unrelated new functionality.

Do not sacrifice clarity or usability for visual novelty.

## 11. Scope

The primary scope is the guest-facing Wedding Memories home page.

Supporting changes may include:

- Shared design tokens.
- Reusable UI components.
- Guest identification presentation.
- Navigation.
- Photo allowance presentation.
- Upload-status presentation.
- Existing application entry points.
- Supporting loading, empty, success and error states.

Other application pages may receive minimal consistency adjustments when necessary, particularly if they share navigation or common design components.

A complete redesign of the camera, gallery, voice recording or wish submission pages is outside the primary scope unless explicitly required to support the home page changes.

## 12. Technical and Business Constraints

Preserve existing:

- API integrations.
- Data contracts.
- Guest identity mechanisms.
- Guest session behaviour.
- Photo allowance rules.
- Upload persistence and retry mechanisms.
- Routes and deep links.
- Security controls.
- Existing wedding configuration.
- Existing functional capabilities.

Do not hardcode wedding-specific information where it is already supplied through configuration or application data.

The current wedding event is Harissa & Faiz on 28 November 2026 at LeQAMR Glasshall, Melaka.

The current photo allowance is 20 photographs per guest.

These values describe the current event and must not replace existing dynamic configuration.

Changes requiring substantial architectural modifications or new backend functionality must be identified during technical discovery.

Prioritise reliable improvements achievable within the existing architecture.

## 13. Definition of Success

The redesign is successful when:

- A first-time guest understands the purpose of the application immediately.
- Guests can easily capture or contribute memories.
- The camera is clearly identifiable as the primary action.
- Wishes and voice recordings are easy to discover.
- Shared and personal memories are clearly distinguished.
- Guests receive understandable and actionable upload feedback.
- The visual identity remains consistent with the wedding invitation.
- The experience feels premium without appearing overdesigned.
- The interface works comfortably across common mobile screen sizes.
- Existing functionality remains reliable.
- The final application feels like a bespoke wedding experience rather than a generic software product.

The intended design outcome is:

Retain the romance of the wedding invitation while adopting the usability of a modern mobile application.
